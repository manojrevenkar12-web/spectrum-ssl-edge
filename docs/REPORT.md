## Q1. Does self-supervised pretraining help when labels are scarce?

With 10 labels per class, fine-tuning the encoder pretrained on bench recordings changes bench accuracy by +5.2 pts against training from scratch (71.1% → 76.4%). With 1000 labels per class the difference is +1.4 pts. The gain shrinks as labels grow, as reported for SimCLR-style pretraining on I/Q.

## Q2. Does pretraining on unlabelled field data make the classifier hold up under shift?

At 1000 labels per class, on the shifts the field pool contained (multipath, hardware, irregular), field pretraining changes mean accuracy by +1.9 pts against supervised training; bench-only pretraining by +1.2 pts. On shifts no pretraining data contained (oversampling, heldout), the changes are +1.7 pts and +0.6 pts.

## Q3. Does calibration survive shift? (Ovadia et al., 2019)

On the bench, temperature scaling takes the supervised model's ECE from 1.8% to 1.7%. Under shift (mean over multipath, hardware, oversampling, irregular, heldout) its ECE is 21.7% against 21.4% without it: the bench temperature no longer helps under shift, but calibration error under shift is 12.9× the bench value. The 3-model ensemble has ECE 15.7% under shift (18.1% with a temperature) — better than the single temperature-scaled model, as Ovadia et al. (2019) found.
